## ✅ DEFENSIBLE CLAIMS (Used on site)

### 1. Sublingual delivery bypasses first-pass metabolism
**Claim:** "Sublingual delivery enters through the lingual venous drainage, bypassing hepatic first-pass metabolism entirely."

**Evidence:**
- Patel VF et al. "Mucosal Drug Delivery." *J Control Release.* 2011;153(2):106–116.
- Rathbone MJ, Hadgraft J. "Absorption of drugs from the human oral cavity." *Int J Pharm.* 1991;74:9–24.

**Status:** ✅ **TRUE** - Well-established pharmacological principle. Sublingual absorption enters systemic circulation via lingual and facial veins, avoiding portal circulation.

---

### 2. Vitamin D₃ shows ~2× bioavailability improvement (sublingual nanoemulsion)
**Claim:** "In patients with IBD, buccal spray achieved comparable efficacy at approximately half the dose (1,143 IU/day vs 2,000 IU/day), suggesting roughly 2× improved bioavailability."

**Evidence:**
- Kojecký V, et al. "Improved bioavailability of buccal nanoemulsion vitamin D compared to conventional oral supplementation in patients with inflammatory bowel disease: a randomized controlled trial." *Front. Med.* 2025;12:1649677.
- Study: n=120 IBD patients, 12-16 weeks, equal 25OHD increase at half the dose

**Status:** ✅ **TRUE** - Published RCT in 2025, specifically in IBD patients with malabsorption

**Caveat:** Effect most pronounced in malabsorption conditions. Healthy individuals show smaller differences.

---

### 3. B₁₂ sublingual = oral (no significant difference)
**Claim:** "Meta-analysis found sublingual and oral B₁₂ equally effective in raising serum cobalamin levels. No statistically significant difference (p=0.270)."

**Evidence:**
- "Efficacy of sublingual and oral vitamin B12 versus intramuscular administration: insights from a systematic review and meta-analysis." *PMC* 12757266, December 2025.
- 16 studies, n=6,098 participants
- Mean % increase: IM ~307%, Oral ~285%, Sublingual ~199%
- No statistical significance between routes (p=0.270)

**Status:** ✅ **TRUE** - Most comprehensive meta-analysis to date. Oral may actually be slightly better than sublingual for B₁₂.

---

### 4. NMN sublingual shows faster metabolite appearance (but same NAD⁺)
**Claim:** "Sublingual NMN increased terminal catabolites (2PY, 4PY) more rapidly than oral administration in the first 60 minutes. However, no significant difference in circulating NMN, NAM, or NAD⁺ levels."

**Evidence:**
- "Sublingual NMN administration increases early circulating terminal catabolites 2PY and 4PY compared with oral administration in healthy adult men." *Sci Rep.* 2026 Jun 16;16(1):27464.
- Crossover RCT, n=14 healthy males
- iAUC higher for 2PY/4PY sublingual, but NMN/NAM/NAD⁺ unchanged

**Status:** ✅ **TRUE** - Shows pharmacokinetic difference (speed) but not bioequivalence difference (total absorption).

---

### 5. Sublingual absorption speed: 2-5 minutes vs 30+ minutes oral
**Claim:** "First measurable blood levels appear within 2-5 minutes" (sublingual) vs "30+ minutes" (oral capsules)

**Evidence:**
- Sohi H et al. "Permeation enhancers in oral mucosa." *Drug Dev Ind Pharm.* 2010;36(3):254–82.
- Standard pharmacology: sublingual Tmax typically 5-15 min, oral Tmax 30-120 min depending on formulation

**Status:** ✅ **TRUE** - Well-documented for various compounds. Primary advantage is SPEED, not total bioavailability.

## ❌ REMOVED CLAIMS (No longer on site)

### 1. Melatonin 4.3× improvement ❌
**Original claim:** "~15% oral → ~65% sublingual = 4.3× fold-change"

**Why removed:** 
- No published RCT directly comparing sublingual vs oral melatonin with measured blood levels
- The "~15% oral bioavailability" figure is from Andersen LP 2016, but that study didn't test sublingual
- In-vitro permeability data ≠ clinical bioequivalence

**Status:** ❌ **UNSUPPORTED** - Removed from Science page

---

### 2. CoQ10 12.7× improvement ❌
**Original claim:** "~3% oral → ~38% sublingual = 12.7× fold-change"

**Why removed:**
- No published clinical trial showing 12.7× improvement for sublingual CoQ10
- Most sublingual CoQ10 studies show 1.5-2× improvement at best
- Formulation matters more than route (ubiquinol vs ubiquinone)

**Status:** ❌ **UNSUPPORTED** - Removed from Science page

---

### 3. Methylcobalamin (B₁₂) 14× improvement ❌
**Original claim:** "~2% oral → ~28% sublingual = 14× fold-change"

**Why removed:**
- Directly contradicted by 2025 meta-analysis showing NO difference
- Sublingual B₁₂ actually performed WORSE than oral in pooled analysis (199% vs 285%)

**Status:** ❌ **FALSE** - Contradicted by latest research, removed

---

### 4. Curcumin 15×+ improvement ❌
**Original claim:** "<1% oral → ~12% sublingual = 15×+ fold-change"

**Why removed:**
- No published sublingual curcumin RCT with blood level measurements
- Curcumin's poor bioavailability is solved by liposomal/micellar formulations, not route
- Piperine (black pepper extract) increases oral absorption by 2000% - more effective than sublingual

**Status:** ❌ **UNSUPPORTED** - Removed from Science page
